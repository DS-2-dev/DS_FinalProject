Does time online make people better or worse at spotting AI images?

Participants judged 20 images as AI-generated or human-made and reported how many hours a week they spend online. We tested whether internet exposure predicts their accuracy.

Subgroup classifiers

We also trained a small logistic model per age-range × gender group to flag low scorers (score ≤ 60%) from time online and age. Most groups are tiny, so these numbers are exploratory only. An ROC AUC around 0.5 means the model does no better than chance.

Method and caveats

  • Data: Google Forms survey export, cleaned and anonymized (names replaced by hashes). One missing hours value was filled with the median.
  • Outcome: share of the 20 image questions answered correctly.
  • Main test: ordinary least squares, score ~ hours + gender + age, with robust standard errors, plus Pearson and Spearman correlations.
  • Limits: self-reported hours, a convenience sample, and an observational design. These results describe associations, not causes. The subgroup analyses run many comparisons on small groups with no correction.
